RCSA — Robotic Crop Stage Assessment

This project asks whether an autonomous field robot can decide, on its own, which crops are ready to harvest. The platform is a farm-ng AMIGA carrying a rig of three cameras, driven down rows of cabbage, broccoli and peppers. The work is split into two tracks, each attacking a different half of that question.

The first track asks what a crop is and what stage it has reached, treating each video frame as an isolated image for a vision-language model to judge. The second track asks where each plant sits in space and how big it is, rebuilding the field as 3D geometry with one bounding box per plant. Harvestability needs both: a ripeness judgement, and a physical location the machine can act on.

Neither track is finished, and both are documented here with their real numbers rather than their best ones. The pages below cover what was built, which libraries were chosen and why, and what each approach cannot yet do.

Pages

  • 3D Reconstruction — turning three-camera rig footage into an interactive 3D field of per-plant bounding boxes.
  • Improving It — what the next capture should change — camera overlap, stereo depth, hardware sync and calibration — and what each currently costs.
  • VLM Baseline — benchmarking three vision-language models on identifying crop type and growth stage from single frames.

The footage

Every result here comes from the same source: three synchronized clips per crop, captured by the AMIGA at 640×480 and 15 frames per second. Two cameras sit at ground level inside the canopy facing roughly opposite directions, and a third looks straight down at the bed. That arrangement turns out to shape the entire engineering problem, for reasons the reconstruction page explains.
